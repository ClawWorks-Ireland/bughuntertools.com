Picking where to start matters more than most people admit. The platform you choose affects the competition you face, the quality of triage you get, and how quickly you get paid and build reputation. Here's an honest comparison.

## HackerOne

HackerOne is the largest bug bounty platform by number of programs and total payouts. That's both its strength and its weakness for beginners.

The upside: brand recognition, the broadest selection of programs, and the largest disclosed report archive. Reading disclosed reports on HackerOne is one of the best ways to learn what good submissions look like. The platform also has a well-established reputation system that unlocks private program invitations.

The downside for beginners: competition is intense. Popular public programs get hit constantly. Finding valid bugs on well-known tech companies' HackerOne programs as a new hunter is genuinely hard. You're competing with hunters who've been doing this for years and know the programs inside out.

Many HackerOne programs are US-headquartered tech companies. If you're an EU-based hunter, some programs have payout restrictions or slower communication simply due to timezone and organizational structure.

Start on HackerOne if: you want access to the widest selection, you're patient about building reputation slowly, and you want to use the disclosed reports as a learning resource.

## Intigriti

My recommendation for most beginners is Intigriti. Here's why.

Intigriti is EU-focused with a strong base of European programs — companies like Colruyt Group, bpost, and a range of fintech and media companies. EU programs on Intigriti tend to have less competition than equivalent programs on HackerOne, partly because the EU hunter community is smaller and partly because American hunters don't always target EU programs.

The triage quality on Intigriti is noticeably better than average. Intigriti employs their own triage team that handles first-pass review for many managed programs. This means you're less likely to get a rejection because someone who doesn't understand the technical impact reviewed your report. For beginners who haven't yet learned to write bulletproof reports, better triage reduces the frustration of valid bugs getting closed on technicalities.

Response times are generally faster than industry average. Knowing whether your report was accepted within a week matters when you're starting out and trying to calibrate whether your work is correct.

The platform's beginner-oriented leaderboards and Hall of Fame are small things, but they help motivation when you're submitting valid bugs that pay modestly.

One honest limitation: the program selection is smaller than HackerOne. If you want to target specific large US tech companies, Intigriti may not have them.

## YesWeHack

YesWeHack is also EU-based and growing. The platform has expanded significantly from its French origins into broader European coverage. Program selection is still smaller than HackerOne and Intigriti, but the competition for individual programs is lower.

YesWeHack runs the European Bug Bounty Conference (BugBountyEU) and has a strong community presence in France, Germany, and the Nordics. If you're in those regions, the local community aspect is a genuine differentiator.

The triage experience varies more program-to-program than Intigriti, because a higher proportion of YesWeHack programs handle their own triage rather than going through a managed service.

Start on YesWeHack if: you're in continental Europe, want less competition than Intigriti, or are specifically targeting French and German enterprise programs.

## Bugcrowd

Bugcrowd is the other major US platform alongside HackerOne. The program selection is similar — enterprise software, financial services, healthcare technology companies.

The Bugcrowd CrowdStream (public disclosure feed) is useful for learning, similar to HackerOne's disclosed reports. The platform has a VRT (Vulnerability Rating Taxonomy) that standardizes how findings are classified, which reduces some ambiguity around severity.

Competition is slightly lower than HackerOne for equivalent programs, mostly because HackerOne's community is larger. But "slightly lower" is still high for popular programs.

Bugcrowd University (free learning resources) is worth reading even if you don't end up focusing on Bugcrowd as your primary platform.

## Which platform to actually start on

For most beginners in 2026: **Intigriti first**.

The reasoning is practical. Lower competition on many programs means you're more likely to find valid bugs in your first months, which builds confidence and demonstrates your approach is working. Better managed triage means fewer demoralizing rejections of valid findings. EU programs often have interesting scope on fintech and e-commerce companies, which have rich API and authentication surfaces.

Once you have 3-5 valid submissions on Intigriti and understand what a good report looks like, adding HackerOne makes sense. The disclosed reports archive is invaluable, and some programs only exist there.

Don't spread yourself across all four platforms simultaneously. The time you spend managing accounts and learning each platform's interface is time you're not spending finding bugs.

## Practical tips for all platforms

Read 20-30 disclosed reports before submitting your first report anywhere. Not to copy findings, but to understand how good hunters frame impact, structure reproduction steps, and handle edge cases in their write-ups.

Start with programs that have smaller scopes. A program with "*.bigcorp.com" as scope is tempting but overwhelming. A program with 2-3 specific applications in scope is more manageable when you're learning.

Check the program's average response time and payout history before targeting it. Both HackerOne and Intigriti surface this data. Programs that average 180-day response times are demoralizing to work with as a beginner.

The platforms don't matter as much as the programs. A bad program on any platform wastes your time. A well-run program with fair triage and honest payouts makes the platform secondary.
